My Website is a Shifting House Next to a River of Knowledge

Laurel explores the idea that a website can be a very personal creative space, and that the artist’s world building skills lend to web design quite well. I personally liked the idea that something like a portfolio site can work along with your work, opposed to just displaying it. She then goes on to list off a bunch of fun physical metaphors for the web. Thinking about the web in this way makes things feel less daunting and permanent. I especially liked the metaphor of a site as a shelf, the idea that nothing is too heavy and can be easily exchanged.



A Rant About "Technology"

A Rant About Technology discusses how the idea of technology tends to be forgotten or skewed to only think about the newest and modern. When I think of technology the first thing I think of is things like IPhone or AI. The author reminds the reader that even the most mundane, everyday objects are considered technology as well. This is kind of a refreshing idea and makes me a bit more appreciative of the skill required to create almost anything.



A Handmade Web

Handmade web refers to a time in between the academic web and the corporate/ commercialized web. I personally have been getting more and more exhausted by the modern state of the web. So it’s kind of nice to imagine going back to an online environment like this. While reading this I was thinking that I was quite surprised no one has created an are.na equivalent to social media using something less algorithmic with a time based feed.



Against an Increasingly User-Hostile Web

The first thing I found interesting in this reading was the sort of unintentional way the web was created. I definitely agree with everything the author discussed and the idea that the user has become just a data point. Things like data harvesting and selling are very concerning both from a security and morality standpoint. The biggest thing I noticed was the lack of autonomy the user has on the web these days. Whether it’s algorithms, advertising, cookies, data harvesting everything is now out of the users control with little ways around it.



The Internet’s Back-to-the-Land Movement

I liked the thinking behind finding alternatives to the big corporate data center that use tons of physical resources. Obviously data center construction is a large debate/ talking point right now, and it’s nice to see some alternative thinking on this topic. I feel like opinions on data centers are very all or nothing, when in reality we do need places to store our data and I think finding ways to do so outside of the current situation is important.
